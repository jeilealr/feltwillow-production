## 1. Select the mode

Choose the mode from the latest explicit owner request and current scoped design contract before building, evaluating or running image-comparison helpers. A user may change the objective from replica to interpretation. Record that decision rather than pretending all prior matching constraints still apply. When ambiguous, ask one consequential question; until resolved, preserve existing approved outputs.

| Mode | Role of existing imagery | Acceptance focus |
|---|---|---|
| `plate_match` | Specific camera/composition/landmark targets, plus style | Silhouette/landmark/projection correspondence and felt quality; use environment_image_matching.md |
| `reference_inspired` | Mood, palette family, narrative identity, craft materials and selected invariants | A beautiful, readable handmade world that supports the story; no mandatory old-image overlay/landmark error |
| `original_design` | Optional research/look references; new environment brief | Brief, style, performance, interaction and continuity review; no invented legacy target |

This is not a quality-level setting. All three modes require convincing craft materials, useful geometry, coherent scale and review. Do not use reference-inspired mode to excuse a sparse blockout. Do not use mandatory plate overlays to penalize a deliberately improved composition.

Use assets/templates/environment-direction.json. `scripts/environment_direction.py` validates only its structural safety rules. A text field claiming owner direction does not authenticate approval.

## 2. Define freedom and invariants

Keep character canonical identity, anatomy, materials and agreed physical ratios unchanged. Keep the fable's causality, interaction needs, entrances/exits, support, occlusion and prop state. Preserve existing approved 2D files, voices and production work. Treat a new environment as a separately versioned candidate.

In reference-inspired mode, explicitly permit appropriate changes such as tree silhouette refinement, root routes, rock distribution, ground contours, foliage families/density, distant scenery, cloud design, camera framing and art-directed lighting. Do not infer permission to remove a story-critical stream, root, doorway or crossing. A specific contact root can change shape only while retaining a tested usable action, and only inside the approved candidate scope.

Distinguish narrative invariants (a grand old tree, an open resting clearing, a route toward Leo) from exact pixel positions. The old reference is not automatically a scale ruler or a promise that every apparent perspective relation has a physically unique 3D answer.

## 3. Richness at useful scales

Design large forms, medium forms and fine surfaces together, not as an object-count contest.

- Large: one hero tree/root identity, an inviting clearing, a readable path and layered distant silhouettes. Reserve quiet negative space around small actors.
- Medium: a few varied felt leaf families, wool moss cushions, compressed grassy banks, asymmetric rock silhouettes, worn path edges and coherent root growth. Avoid tree-ball rows and a carpet of identical polygon chips.
- Fine: short, attached fibers; soft surface irregularity; mottled color and material-specific micro-relief. Use enough thickness and screen size to survive delivery resolution, filtering and motion. More noise is not more craft.

Distribute detail deliberately: foreground accents, hero interaction area, midground foliage masses and lower-detail distance. Keep the main action readable. Richness and calm are compatible; most detail remains static.

Build a small reusable craft kit before scattering it: one convincing moss cushion, two or three leaf/grass shapes, a rock finish, a bark/root finish and a path-edge transition. This is a suggested minimal kit, not a universal quota. Judge those samples at the expected camera distances before populating the entire set.

Do not substitute realistic bark scans, wet soil or long mammal fur for the felt medium by default. References can provide structural ideas, but each visible surface must belong to the same miniature craft world. Sparse fuzzy silhouettes may be useful; a uniform furry coating on everything is not.

## 4. Run a bounded creative pass

1. Save the existing candidate and record source hashes. Resolve linked libraries and local changes without overwriting them.
2. Write the selected mode, owner direction, retained story constraints, allowable changes, output revision, camera envelope and spend/render limits.
3. Choose one art direction in prose or a small number of low-cost layout variants only if useful. Do not generate an unlimited concept search.
4. Establish at least an establishing view, a medium actor view and the hardest contact/root view. Use approved assets or clearly labeled physical-scale proxies.
5. Test one hero material region on actual curved geometry and a silhouette edge. Inspect neutral and story illumination at intended delivery size.
6. Improve hero form, material differentiation and layered dressing. Render fixed-camera before/after evidence of the new design. The old reference remains a style comparison, not a mandatory geometric overlay.
7. Evaluate stills and a short motion/ambient preview. Stop at the agreed limit; change method if the same defect persists. Candidate review is not automatic publication.

The material test may precede small composition refinements in this mode. Do not spend most of the pass reducing obsolete pixel-coordinate errors while the tree and path still look like generic CG.

## 5. Material proof and review

Read material-evidence.md before interpreting 'PATINA used'. Audit the saved nodes and material assignments, then actual visible effect. Keep material design distinct from texture-provider selection; procedural material work can succeed, and paid maps can fail.

Review independently: craft/felt recognition; focal hierarchy; hero identity; near/mid/far layering; surface differentiation; scale/contact/route; lighting/exposure; view envelope; temporal stability; performance; provenance. A mean aesthetic score cannot cancel failed support or bad character identity.

For plate-match mode add explicit source correspondence. For reference-inspired mode compare the candidate to its own approved design brief, retained invariants and the art-direction reference. Use an intentional-change ledger instead of treating every difference as a defect.

## 6. Publish a world, preserve continuity

Blender enables reusable geometry; it does not automatically prevent an agent from editing a linked mesh, changing a seed, lens, light, LOD or material. Publish an immutable environment revision after review. Record scene links, asset/material recipe hashes, hero transforms, authored seeds, camera setups, lighting variants and a shared story clock.

Freeze geography after scoped design approval, not before exploration. Evolve by new versions; do not silently mutate already used libraries. Keep day/dusk as lighting variants of the same geometry unless a new world event changes it.

Do not mix a redesigned Blender Great Tree and old image/Wan versions within one apparent uninterrupted location without explicit review. Choose a route: retain old imagery for the existing cut and keep the new set experimental; create reviewed new image plates from the approved new set; or approve a deliberate visual transition. Shared names alone do not ensure compatible geography or color. A frozen Blender guide also does not guarantee that generated Wan pixels obey it.

## 7. S05 application and transfer to future fables

Current user direction authorizes exploring a richer reference-inspired S05 environment candidate, not replacing every approved v6 image. Read the local current scene to establish the actual approach, rest and contact requirements. Preserve Leo and future Milo physical scale. Keep an open interaction area; improve tree/roots, matted ground/path edges, moss, leaf clusters and distant depth with deliberate detail hierarchy.

The reference image's grandeur, cozy handmade detail and light/color relationships remain relevant; exact rock coordinates, cloud contours and prior calibration residuals need not dominate the candidate's score. Record intentional changes.

For birds, validate perches, wing clearance and landing paths. For people, doorways, reach/seating and graspable props. For swimmers, bank continuity and waterlines. Reuse the same design-mode rule rather than baking Leo/Milo assumptions into every world.
